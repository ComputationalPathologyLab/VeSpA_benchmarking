\section{Results}

Table~\ref{tab:summary_metrics} reports the mean segmentation performance across the four benchmark histological images for all four methods. VeSpA achieved the best mean Dice coefficient (0.7332) and IoU (0.5804). The hybrid VeSpA-SAM method ranked second with a mean Dice coefficient of 0.6901 and the highest mean precision (0.8839), indicating that stain-guided prompting substantially reduced false positives relative to the generic SAM baseline. However, the hybrid method remained slightly below VeSpA in overlap-based metrics because its recall (0.5732) was lower than VeSpA's 0.6712.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering

\label{tab:summary_metrics}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\hline
Model & Dice & IoU & Precision & Recall \\
\hline
VeSpA & 0.7332 & 0.5804 & 0.8171 & 0.6712 \\
VeSpA+SAM (Hybrid) & 0.6901 & 0.5295 & 0.8839 & 0.5732 \\
SAM & 0.2950 & 0.1754 & 0.6509 & 0.1914 \\
YOLOv8-seg & 0.0299 & 0.0154 & 0.8567 & 0.0156 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The hybrid method produced substantial gains over automatic SAM on all four images, raising Dice from 0.3385 to 0.5991 on \texttt{ROI\_1}, from 0.1574 to 0.6917 on \texttt{ROI\_2}, from 0.3424 to 0.7212 on \texttt{ROI\_3}, and from 0.3415 to 0.7483 on \texttt{ROI\_4}. Despite these improvements, VeSpA remained the top-performing method on every image in this benchmark.

Runtime measurements are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:runtime}. YOLOv8-seg remained the fastest method at 0.63 s per image, followed by VeSpA at 2.58 s. The hybrid VeSpA-SAM method required 43.46 s per image on average, making it the slowest configuration in the current repository benchmark because it runs prompt-guided SAM refinement over multiple stain-derived components per image.

VeSpA & 2.58 \\

\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The plots confirm that the hybrid pipeline closes much of the gap between baseline SAM and VeSpA while clearly outperforming YOLOv8-seg in overlap-based metrics. Qualitative overlays saved under \path{results/prediction/} show that the hybrid method is more conservative than VeSpA, with higher precision but lower recall on several images.
