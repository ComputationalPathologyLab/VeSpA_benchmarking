\section{Conclusion}
Within the current repository benchmark, the custom VeSpA pipeline remained the best-performing method for vessel segmentation in the available histological images, achieving the highest mean Dice (0.7332) and IoU (0.5804) while remaining computationally efficient at 2.58 s per image. The hybrid VeSpA-SAM pipeline ranked second with a mean Dice of 0.6901 and the highest mean precision (0.8839), showing that stain-guided prompting can recover much of the performance gap between generic SAM and a domain-tuned classical pipeline. However, the hybrid method was slower than both VeSpA and automatic SAM, requiring 43.46 s per image on average in the current implementation.

These findings should be interpreted within the scope of the implemented experiment. The dataset contains four images, and neither SAM nor YOLOv8-seg was fine-tuned for histological vessel segmentation in this repository. Accordingly, the reported results demonstrate comparative behavior of the current codebase rather than a general ranking for these model families. Future work could reduce the hybrid runtime by pruning prompts, merging nearby components before SAM refinement, or batching prompts more efficiently; additional work could also extend the benchmark with larger curated histology datasets and supervised fine-tuning of YOLO segmentation on task-matched annotations. In the broader Spatial Vessel Analysis framework described on the project website, improved segmentation quality would support more reliable downstream vessel statistics and spatial analyses.
